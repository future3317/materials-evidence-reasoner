# Benchmark and Regression Protocol

A benchmark is not only a prompt. It requires versioned source artifacts, adjudicated gold records, scoring rules, and reproducible execution.

## 1. Required Assets

Each benchmark release contains:

- immutable source files or stable source identifiers and hashes;
- source-access and license status;
- a corpus manifest with inclusion, exclusion, and deduplication decisions;
- field-contract and schema versions;
- independent annotations and adjudicated gold JSON;
- an annotation decision log for ambiguous cases;
- deterministic structural and reference validation;
- extraction and routing metrics;
- run configuration, model or tool version, date, and random seed when relevant;
- failure records rather than only successful examples.

An LLM may propose annotations or identify possible errors. It must not be the only author or judge of gold data.

### 1.1 Version 4.2 machine assets

- `adapter-registry.json` declares adapter layer, ruleset version, implementation status and reference path.
- `adapter-field-contracts.json` is generated from the backtick-delimited field declarations in adapter references by `scripts/build_field_contracts.py`.
- `adapter-benchmark-cases.json` is generated by `scripts/build_adapter_benchmarks.py` and contains two positive, two negative and two ambiguous synthetic routing cases per adapter.
- `scripts/validate_output.py --self-test` checks schema validity, IDs, references, adapter status/version boundaries, record-type and field vocabulary, artifact-analysis lineage and benchmark coverage.

Synthetic cases are unit-style routing regressions, not a source-backed gold corpus. Keep `review_status: provisional` until a human reviewer checks the expected route and boundary rationale. Human review of synthetic cases still does not replace immutable real sources with independently annotated and adjudicated gold JSON.

## 2. Corpus Design

Build the corpus around failure modes, not keyword coverage alone.

### 2.1 Core materials subset

Include multiple material classes and source formats:

- battery or electrochemical materials;
- metals or alloys;
- polymers or composites;
- ceramics, glass, cement, or solid electrolytes;
- semiconductors, thin films, interfaces, or photovoltaic materials;
- at least one patent, standard, TDS, spreadsheet, or technical report when those formats are in scope.

Include cases with:

- one paper and one material entity;
- one paper and multiple compositions or process variants;
- identical composition with different processing or states;
- main text and supplement with local-condition overrides;
- tables, curves, figures, OCR, and missing full text;
- reported, derived, approximate, conflicting, negative, invalid, and inaccessible values;
- incompatible units, denominators, methods, geometries, temperatures, pressures, fields, or analysis windows;
- background and cited materials that must not leak into the target entity;
- invalid file extensions or web payloads masquerading as documents.

### 2.2 Adapter-routing subset

For every domain adapter include:

- clear positive sources;
- clear negative sources containing misleading vocabulary;
- ambiguous candidate sources;
- target versus control or apparatus scope traps;
- experimental versus theoretical or device-profile boundaries;
- negative-results sources;
- cross-domain sources that should load more than one adapter.

### 2.3 Splits

Use document-level train/development/test separation. Keep source families, preprint-to-journal versions, supplementary files, and near-duplicate papers in the same split to prevent leakage.

Do not tune rules on the held-out test set. Add newly discovered failure modes to a future versioned release.

## 3. Annotation Workflow

1. Freeze source files, hashes, schema, adapter versions, and annotation instructions.
2. Resolve source identity and target `entity_scope` before property annotation.
3. Have two annotators independently label a representative high-risk subset.
4. Compare entity boundaries, records, values, conditions, locators, status, and adapter decisions.
5. Adjudicate disagreements with a domain expert or documented rule.
6. Record why the final label was chosen and whether the rule needs revision.
7. Validate adjudicated JSON with `scripts/validate_output.py`.
8. Lock gold files as read-only release artifacts.

Annotation tools may highlight likely missing or erroneous fields, but annotators must return to the source. Existing extraction output is never evidence for itself.

## 4. Gold Annotation Requirements

Every gold record must satisfy:

- target entity and scope are explicit;
- source evidence is locatable;
- raw values, symbols, units, ranges, and criteria are preserved;
- normalized values include transformations and input evidence;
- meaning-changing conditions attach to the correct record;
- reported, derived, inferred, secondary, ambiguous, conflicting, and invalid states remain distinct;
- missing information is recorded when it affects decisions;
- background, controls, apparatus, and cited comparisons remain outside target scope;
- adapter decisions include source IDs, target entities, matched signals, gates, exclusions, and reasons;
- candidate or skipped adapters have no domain records.

Do not force annotators to fill optional fields without evidence. Gold omission means the field does not apply or is not required; consequential absence belongs in `missing_information[]`.

## 5. Error Taxonomy

Use core audit codes and classify scoring errors at least as:

- `missing-record`: a gold entity, run, property, evidence, or domain record is absent;
- `unsupported-record`: output has no adequate source support;
- `wrong-value`: raw or normalized value is incorrect;
- `wrong-unit`: raw or normalized unit is incorrect;
- `wrong-condition`: condition value is wrong;
- `condition-misattachment`: correct condition is attached to the wrong entity or result;
- `wrong-entity`: record is bound to the wrong material, sample, phase, or run;
- `entity-merge`: distinct entities were collapsed;
- `scope-leak`: background, control, apparatus, or cited material entered target records;
- `wrong-status`: reported, derived, inferred, negative, ambiguous, or conflicting state is wrong;
- `wrong-locator`: page, section, figure, table, row, or panel is wrong or unverifiable;
- `normalization-error`: conversion, denominator, formula, or canonical mapping is wrong;
- `duplicate-record`: one fact is counted more than once;
- `adapter-false-positive`: adapter loaded when it should not;
- `adapter-false-negative`: adapter failed to load when it should;
- `adapter-candidate-error`: uncertain source was forced to load or skip;
- `schema-error`: JSON structure or references are invalid.

For review reports, show only problematic fields, but preserve the complete corrected JSON as a separate artifact.

## 6. Scoring Units

Report metrics at multiple levels. Do not hide failures inside one overall score.

### 6.1 Structural validity

- JSON parse rate;
- JSON Schema pass rate;
- unique-ID pass rate;
- reference-integrity pass rate;
- adapter-boundary pass rate.

### 6.2 Routing

For each adapter report a three-class confusion matrix for `load`, `candidate`, and `skip`.

Also report:

- load precision and recall;
- candidate recall for ambiguous cases;
- false-load rate caused by background or apparatus mentions;
- entity-binding accuracy for loaded adapters;
- profile accuracy for experimental, theoretical, device, review, and mixed sources.

### 6.3 Entity and lineage

- entity detection precision, recall, and F1;
- entity-type accuracy;
- parent-child and process-lineage accuracy;
- entity-merge and scope-leak rates.

Match entities using adjudicated identity, composition, state, process, and scope, not name alone.

### 6.4 Records and fields

For a set of predicted records `P` and gold records `G`:

```text
precision = true_positive / (true_positive + false_positive)
recall    = true_positive / (true_positive + false_negative)
F1        = 2 * precision * recall / (precision + recall)
```

Report record-level and field-level micro and macro averages. A record match requires compatible entity, record type, property or field identity, and meaning-changing conditions.

### 6.5 Values and units

Report separately:

- exact raw-value accuracy;
- normalized-value accuracy;
- unit accuracy;
- approximation, inequality, and range preservation;
- derivation correctness;
- uncertainty preservation.

Define numerical tolerance by property and measurement resolution before testing. Never invent one tolerance for all materials properties.

### 6.6 Conditions

Measure:

- condition detection precision and recall;
- condition-value accuracy;
- attachment accuracy to entity, measurement, and property;
- blocker recall for meaning-changing mismatches;
- silent-default rate for invented ambient pressure, zero field, room temperature, or other defaults.

### 6.7 Evidence

Measure:

- source attribution accuracy;
- evidence precision and recall;
- locator exact match and relaxed match;
- direct versus secondary claim classification;
- unsupported-claim rate;
- correct evidence-to-entity attachment.

Use relaxed locator matching only for equivalent page numbering, HTML/PDF mapping, or overlapping text spans. It must not excuse the wrong figure, table, sample, or source.

### 6.8 Missingness and confidence

Measure missing-reason accuracy and whether decision-critical missing fields were surfaced.

For confidence, evaluate whether the categorical level follows its observable basis rules. Do not score an uncalibrated probability. If numeric confidence is introduced later, calibrate it on held-out data and report reliability curves.

### 6.9 Repeatability

Run each stochastic workflow at least twice with the same inputs. Report:

- adapter-decision agreement;
- entity and record set agreement;
- value and condition agreement;
- evidence-locator agreement;
- variation in unsupported claims.

Repeatability does not establish correctness; report it alongside gold accuracy.

## 7. Release Gates

The following are hard gates for a benchmarked release:

- all released JSON parses and passes the declared schema;
- no unresolved duplicate IDs or broken references;
- no domain records from `candidate` or `skip` adapters;
- no fabricated evidence in critical fixtures;
- no target-entity leakage in critical scope fixtures;
- all derived values identify their input evidence and operation;
- processed or fitted data artifacts identify their analysis step, and each analysis step identifies its input and output artifacts;
- measurement and simulation results preserve raw/calibrated/processed/fitted level rather than collapsing all files into one undifferentiated reference;
- all benchmark sources and gold records carry version identities.

An adapter may remain `specified` while its human-readable reference, field contract and synthetic routing tests are complete. Promote it to `implemented` only after its source-backed gold subset and extraction tests have been reviewed; `specified`, `candidate` and `skip` routes must not emit that adapter's `domain_records[]`.

Set numerical precision, recall, and F1 targets only after measuring a representative baseline. Keep a metric vector rather than prematurely optimizing one aggregate score.

## 8. General Regression Cases

At minimum test that:

1. same composition with different processing becomes separate entities or runs;
2. document defaults do not override sample-specific conditions;
3. best-reported values do not become universal baselines;
4. literature, local, and requirement baselines remain separate;
5. invalid payloads are rejected before extraction;
6. missing temperature or protocol blocks invalid comparison;
7. approximate figure values remain approximate and cite the figure;
8. derived values retain formulas and evidence inputs;
9. a failed calibration produces `data-quality-suspect` before a material mechanism;
10. a promising outlier triggers definition and selection audits;
11. a new anomaly does not modify a stable local baseline;
12. unavailable persistence remains `proposed-not-written`;
13. corrected output is valid JSON with no broken references.

## 9. Superconductivity Adapter Regression Cases

Test at least:

1. direct superconductivity wording plus target-bound transport and magnetic evidence loads the adapter;
2. `Tc` meaning Curie or crystallization temperature does not load it;
3. a superconducting magnet used as apparatus produces `skip`;
4. a superconducting electrode outside target scope does not assign superconductivity to the target material;
5. zero resistance without another resolved signal remains candidate when interpretation is unclear;
6. onset and zero temperatures from one curve count as one evidence channel;
7. a theoretical prediction is `predicted-superconducting`, not experimentally reported superconductivity;
8. a negative measurement preserves the lower temperature and other measurement bounds;
9. `Hc` as coercive field and `Jc` as generic current density do not trigger the adapter;
10. missing field, pressure, direction, or criterion is not filled by convention;
11. `Hc2(0)` fitted by WHH is marked derived with fit evidence;
12. mechanism records distinguish measured support, calculation support, proposal, speculation, and conflict;
13. candidate and skip decisions emit no superconductivity domain records.

## 10. Benchmark Report

Publish:

- corpus and split summary;
- schema and adapter versions;
- tool and model configuration;
- structural validity results;
- routing confusion matrices;
- entity, record, field, value, condition, and evidence metrics;
- error counts by taxonomy;
- repeatability results;
- representative corrected failures;
- unresolved annotation disagreements;
- changes from the previous benchmark version.

Keep raw model output, validator output, and adjudicated corrections separate so the benchmark remains auditable.
